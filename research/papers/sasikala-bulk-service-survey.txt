S. Sasikala1 , K. Indhira2
1,2 School of Advanced Sciences

Abstract:

In Bulk service queueing models, customers are served in batches, the batches

may be of fixed length or variable length. Sometimes, the service rate may depend on the
number of customers waiting for service. The motivation of this model rise from manufacturing
systems, elevators, transport vehicles etc. In this survey, we provide an overview about bulk
service queueing models.
Key Words:

bulk service, setup time, vacations, N-policy, breakdown, closedown time,

retrial

1. Introduction
A detailed work about bulk queues can be found in Chaudhry and Templeton
[1]. Bulk service queueing models is originated with Bailey [2]. He derived the
equilibrium distribution of the queue length by using embedded-Markov chain
technique, he considered fixed-batch service. In fixed-batch service queueing
models, the server always serve fixed number of customers in batch.
Embedded Markov-chain technique was introduced by Kendall [3]. In this
technique, discrete time Markov chain is obtained from non-Markovian process

by using regeneration points. The regeneration points may be service completion epoch, customers departure epoch, customers arrival epoch etc. The
stochastic matrix of the obtained Markov chain can be used for finding the
performance measures of a queueing models.
General bulk service rule was first introduced by Neuts [4]. General bulk
service rule states that the server will start to provide service only when atleast
‘a’ units are present in the queue, and maximum service capacity is ‘b’. On
completion of a batch of service, if less than ‘a’ customers are present in the
queue then server has to wait upto the queue length reaches the value ‘a’. If
less than or equal to ‘b’ and greater than or equal to ‘a’ customers are in the
queue, then all the existing customers are taken into service. If greater than are
equal to ‘b’ customers are in the queue, then ‘b’ customers taken into service.
Downton [5] obtained the waiting time distribution of bulk service queues
by considering random arrivals and random service time distribution. In bulk
service queueing problem Jaiswal [6] conformed the result of Downton. He
derived waiting time distribution using embedded Markov-chain technique. He
assumed that the batches are either fixed queue length or whole queue, whatever
is lower. That is, If more than ‘a’ customers are in the queue, then ‘a’ customers
are taken into service. If lesser than ‘a’ customers are in the queue all the
existing customers are taken into service.
This paper is summarised as follows : In Section 2. reviews the models of
bulk service queues without vacation. In Section 3. is focused on bulk service
queues with vacation. In Section 4. contains tandem queues with bulk service.
In Section 5. concludes with uses of queueing models and some useful future
direction.

Chaudhry and Gupta [11], Gupta and Laxmi [12], Chakravarthy [13], Gupta
and Goswami [14], Laxmi and Gupta [15] analyzed bulk service queueing system with finite queue length. Jaiswal [16] derived the distribution of the queue
length by using embedded-Markov chain technique and Phase method, he compared these two techniques. In phase method, service of a customer consisting
of ‘n’ stages, each stage having exponential service time. Service of a customer is finished, when the customer service is processed all ‘n’ stages. The
overall service time of a customer has n-phase Erlang distribution is a convolution of n same exponential distribution. Jayaraman et al. [17] studied bulk
service queueing system with customers arrival rate depends on server breakdown. Miller [18], Borthakur and Medhi [19], Chaudhry et al. [20], Powell
and Humblet [21], Abolnikov and Dshalalow [22] considered bulk queueing system. Medhi [23], Borthakur [24] investigated M/M (a, b)/1 queueing system.
Finch [25] studied about bulk service with finite capacity with fixed batch service. Powell [26] studied poisson and non-poisson arrivals with bulk service
queueing system. Easton and Chaudhry [27] obtained steady state results for
Ek /M (a, b)/1 queueing system using Erlangian technique. Deb and Serfozo [28]
analyzed optimal control for bath service queueing system.
Goswami et al. [29] analyzed discrete time batch service queueing system
with accessible and non-accessible batches under general bulk service rule. If the
queue length is lesser than minimum threshold level ‘a’, then the server will be
in idle state. If the queue length is ‘n’(a ≤ n ≤ d − 1), then the server will take
all the existing customers into service and late arrivals are allowed to enter the
ongoing service upto accessible limit ‘d’ is reached this is termed as accessible
batch. If the length of the queue is ‘n’ (n ≥ d), then the server will take all the
existing customers into service, and once service started, even if the number of
customers currently in service is lesser than maximum threshold level ‘b’, newly
arriving customers are not allowed to enter in the ongoing service this is termed
as non-accessible batch. Krishnamoorthy and Ushakumari [30] analyzed batch

service queue with single departure and accessible batches. Madan et al. [31]
studied bulk service queue with random breakdown. Unplanned breakdown is
called random breakdown, once breakdown occur the server immediately will
enter into repair process, after repair process it resumes service.
Arumuganathan and Jeyakumar [32] analyzed M x /G(a, c, b)/1 queueing
system. The server can choose either single service or bulk service, this selection depends on the number of customers in the queue, while entering into
the service. The service will start only when ‘a’ customers are in the queue. If
the queue length is ‘a’, the server will choose single service and serves one by
one. If the queue length is ‘c’(c ≥ a), the server will choose bulk service and
serves at most ‘b’ customers in a batch. Alfa and He [33], Selim [34] analyzed
bulk service queueing system with variable service capacity. Zili et al. [35] analyzed bulk service queueing system with priority queue. The server will provide
service for non-priority customers only after the service completion of priority
customers.
Madan [36], Singh and Ram [37] examined bulk service queueing system
with service interruption. Once breakdown occur, the server will send for repair
and the service of the current batch will be suspended. After completion of its
repair, the server will serve suspended batch of customers. Dhas et al. [38]
considered bulk service with preemptive priority queueing system. Among two
queues, one queue is for high priority customers and another queue is for low
priority customers. If the queue length of high priority customers is reached the
minimum capacity of the server, immediately the server will start serve for high
priority customers, otherwise the server will serve for low priority customers.
Banerjee et al.[39, 40], Banerjee and Gupta [41] investigated bulk service
queueing system with batch size dependent service, service time of a batch is
depends upon the batch size. Chang et al. [42], Chaudhry and Chang [43],
Banerjee et al. [44], Germs and Van Foreest [45] analyzed bulk service queue
with finite queue length and variable server capacity. Chang and Choi [46]
studied bulk queue with variable server capacity, finite queue length and setuptimes. Muh [47] investigated M x /G(r, R)/1 queueing system with N-policy
and setuptime. Tadj and Choudhury [48] studied bulk service queueing system with unreliable server. Unpredictable breakdowns will occur in unreliable
server. Haridass et al. studied [49] analyzed bulk service queue with retrial,
arriving customers finds the server is in busy they are grouped is termed as
orbit.

3. Bulk service queues with vacation
3.1. Single server
A detailed survey on server vacation have analyzed by Doshi [60], server vacation models are favourable for the system in which server wants to do secondary job in the idle time. Arumuganathan and Jeyakumar [61] analyzed
M x /G(a, b)/1 queueing model with multiple vacation and closedown time. In
closedown time queueing system, on completion of a service, if the queue length
is lesser than the minimum service capacity of the server, the server performs

closedown work. In M x /G(a, b)/1 queueing system with multiple vacation, on
completion of a service, if the queue size is at-most ‘a − 1’, the server leaves for
a vacation of random length. After a vacation, if the queue length is less than
‘a’, then the server leaves for another vacation and so on, this will continue
until the queue length reaches at least ‘a’. After a vacation, if the queue size
is greater than or equal to ‘a’ say ξ , then the server serves min(ξ, b), where
b ≥ a. Lee et al [62] analyzed fixed-length batch service queue with vacations.
In single vacation queueing model, the server will permit to take exactly one
vacation on completion of it’s service. After a service, if the queue length is
fewer than ‘a’, then the server leaves for a vacation of random length. After
a vacation, if the queue size is more than ‘a’, the server will take into service
exactly ‘a’ customers. If not, he remains idle upto the queue size reaches ‘a’.
Lee et al. [63] analyzed bulk service queue with single vacation.
Arumuganathan and Jeyakumar [64] examined M x /G(a, b)/1 queueing system with multiple vacations, setup times, closedown times and N -policy. Startup
time or setup time is preparatory work of the server before start to provide service. In N -policy queueing system, the server begins service from idle period
only when the queue length reaches at least N (N ≥ 1). A survey on queueing
models with N-policy can be found in Jayachitra and Albert [65]. Jeyakumar
and Senthilnathan [66] examined M x /G(a, b)/1 queueing system with multiple
vacations, setuptimes and server breakdown without interruption. The breakdown condition of the service location, defect status and proper maintenance
are termed as renovation jobs of the server. Arumuganathan and Ramaswami
[67] studied about M x /G(a, b)/1 queueing system with fast and slow services.
The service rate is high, when the queue length is greater than are equal to
N (N ≥ b). The service rate is low, when the queue length is less than N.
Jeyakumar and Senthilnathan [68] studied M x /G(a, b)/1 queueing system with
server breakdown without interruption, multiple vacations and closedown times.
Reddy et al. [69] examined M x /G(a, b)/1 queue with multiple vacation,
setup times and N-policy. Haridass and Arumuganathan [70] investigated vacation interruption with M x /G(a, b)/1 queueing system. During vacation period,
if the queue reaches of length ‘a’, the server will start to provide service immediately. A detailed survey on queues with interruption can be found in Krishnamoorthy et al. [71]. Arumuganathan and Malliga [72] analyzed M x /G(a, b)/1
queueing system with multiple vacation, setuptimes, repair of server on request
by a leaving bunch of customers. On completion of a service, the system either
requires repair with probability π or does not require repair with probability
1− π. Lee et al. [73], Dshalalow and Yellen [74] examined bulk service queueing
system with multiple vacation.

Reddy and Anitha [75] analyzed M/M (a, b)/1 queueing system with delayed
vacation. After a vacation or service, if the queue length is at most ‘a − 1’, the
server will wait idle in the system this is termed as delay time or changeover
time. During this delay time, if there is an arrival occur, the server will start
to provide service else will go for vacation. Jacob and Madhusoodanan [76]
studied M x /G(a, b)/1 queueing system with multiple vacation and finite waiting room. Nadarajan and Subramaniyam [77] studied M/M (a, b)/1 queueing
system with server vacation. Gupta and Sikdar [78] studied bulk service queue
with single vacation. Sikdar [79] analyzed M AP/G(a, b)/1/N queueing system
with multiple vacation. Laxmi et al. [80] examined bulk service renewal input (a, c, b)-policy with multiple vacations and changeover times. Goswami
and Laxmi [81] studied bulk service queue with multiple vacation, accessible
and non accessible batches. Haridass and Arumuganathan [82] analyzed bulk
service queueing system with variant threshold policy for vacation. Tadj et al.
[83] studied quorum queue with setup time, N-policy and Bernoulli vacation.
Bernoulli schedule vacation states that, after each batch of service completion
epoch the server may go for secondary job or continue its service.
Haridass and Arumuganathan [84] analyzed bulk service queue with server
choice of admitting re-service, multiple vacation and setup time. On completion
of a batch of service, the leaving batch of customers may appeal for re-service
and this request for re-service may be accept or may not. Balasubramanian
et al. [85] studied bulk service queueing system with overloading and multiple
vacation. After a service or vacation, the server finds ‘ξ(a ≤ ξ < N )’ customers
waiting for service, then the server serves min(ξ, b) customers in a batch, if the
queue size is greater than ‘N’, then the server will increase its service capacity
called overload and serves ‘N’ customers in a batch. Balasubramanian and Arumuganathan [86] examined bulk queue with modified M-vacation policy, after
completing ‘M’ vacations, if the queue size is lesser than minimum threshold
value, then the server will stay in idle state upto the queue size reaches the
minimum threshold value of the server. Jeyakumar and Arumuganathan [87]
analyzed M/G(a, b)/1 queueing system with M-vacation policy.
Laxmi and Demie [88] analyzed GI/M (a, c, b)/1 queueing system with working vacation and change over time. In bulk service with working vacation
queueing models, instead of completely idle in the secondary job period, the
server will work in that period with different rate. Ayyappan et al. [89] studied
bulk service queueing system with gated service and multiple vacation, once
enter into service with a batch of ‘k’ customers, each time the server will provide service for ‘k’ customers. Jeyakumar and Arumuganathan [90] considered
M x /G(a, b)/1 queueing system with control policy for re-service and multiple

4. Tandem queues with bulk service
A simple queueing systems connected by a series in one direction is called
tandem queue, the service of each queueing system in a tandem queue may
be single or batch. Reddy et al. [97] studied tandem queue with three units,
each having finite number of parallel servers, bulk service only in third unit. In
first unite infinite waiting capacity, other two units has finite waiting capacity
namely ‘M’ and ‘N’. In third unit bulk service will be given by general bulk
service rule. Ayyappan and Velmurugan [98] examined tandem queue with two
units, bulk service in unit two accessible and non accessible batch in unit two.
In first unit customers are served singly, unit two has ‘c’ parallel servers. Dhas
and Sumitha [99] studied tandem queue with two units, new general bulk service
rule for unit two. New bulk service rule states that the server will serve not
lesser than ‘a’ and not higher than ‘b’ in a batch, arriving customers are allowed
to enter into service upto the maximum capacity of the server without affecting
its service time. A tandem queue with two units and random breakdown in
unit two studied by Nadarajan and Dhas [100]. Nadarajan and Jayaraman
[101] analyzed tandem queue with two units, random breakdown in both the
units, multiple vacation in unit two. Reddy et al. [102] analyzed tandem queue
with two types of vacation, service and finite buffer.

5. Conclusion
In this paper we studied the overview of bulk service queueing models, this
models are used to predict the system in advance and we can avoid loss by using
of these models, here we include a few navigation for further research work. A
less number of works are done in the field of bulk service queueing models with
multiserver, bulk service queue with priority queues and bulk service queue
with working vacations, developing good approximations for these models will
be useful future work.
